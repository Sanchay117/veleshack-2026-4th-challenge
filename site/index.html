The arena

Four nodes. Three pools. One battery each.

Every round each edge node splits a budget across compute, energy and security. Pools are shared in proportion to the bids (a Kelly mechanism) and the payoff is a CES utility that rewards balanced bundles. Score is the sum over 60 rounds.

allocation
x = C · b / Σb

Your share follows your bid, with diminishing returns.

Miss a service floor

Too little compute or security and the round is halved. Miss both and it is quartered.

The energy you win drains you

Run your battery flat and you sit rounds out, scoring nothing. The three baseline bots never notice and sleep about a third of the run.

Insight 1

Don't estimate the market. Compute it.

Three facts are public: the bots are open source, they run on a copy of our device, and /v1/swarm publishes every node's battery each round. So each round the agent replays the bots' own logic on the public round data and reproduces their bids to six decimals. The clearing price checks the forecast after every round, and a residual absorbs anything unexplained. With the field known exactly, both service floors are bought at the exact minimum: 2 misses in 9,600 rounds.

Insight 2

Their naps are predictable. Plan around them.

The bots never manage their batteries, so rolling their shadows forward tells us who will be asleep one round ahead 99% of the time. That forecast feeds a dynamic programme over our battery for the rest of the run, solved again every round in about 50 ms: Vt(B) = maxa [ ut(a) + Vt+1(B − da) ]. It sips energy when the pools are crowded, spends it when rivals sleep, rests on purpose when they wake, and empties the battery on the final round.

Conditions it never saw

Battery 50% hungrier: +46.4%. 33% thriftier: +23.9%. An unknown extra agent in the swarm: +38.8%. Joining at round 12: +35.8%. The practice scenario: +35.5%. The drain rate is re-fitted online from the agent's own results.

the honest welfare answer

We mostly take from the bots

With us in the seat instead of the template the bots lose 12.2% of their score and the whole swarm only 1.4%; log social welfare goes from −3.08 to −3.20. Leaving the energy pool to the bots makes them drain faster: they rest 11% more.

Engineering

Built not to lose a round.

functional: 0 missed rounds under injected faults

20 / 20

resilience: 503s, 429s, latency, lease expiry

  • Deadline-aware retries. Injected 503s are independent dice rolls, so the bid path backs off with full jitter from 60 ms and keeps trying until the round actually closes, never sleeping past it.
  • A bug in the template, found live. Its loop asks only for the result of the round it has just bid in, which has not settled yet, so it read 1 result in 49 rounds. Ours reads them all, and the opponent model learns which stale result the bots, running the same loop, are really using.
  • Never over budget. Six-decimal rounding could tip a bid 1.5e-6 over the arena's tolerance, a compromise penalty. The unit tests caught it; bids now round down.
  • Fallbacks at every layer. No swarm snapshot: estimate from prices. Forecast drifting: widen the floor margins with the measured error. Planner error: a safe battery-aware bid. A strategy bug costs score, never the run.
